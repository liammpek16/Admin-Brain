## Important: the Von Koschembahr line is a step-line
Frederick von Koschembahr's 1941 Declaration of Intention lists his only child as **"von Nemirowitsch-Dantschenko, Georg (stepson)"**, born 6 Oct 1921 in Munich. So George was Frederick's stepson, and the family took the Von Koschembahr name through Anna's second marriage, on 24 Dec 1929 in Dresden.

Your **biological** line on this side runs through Anna and George's birth father. He was most likely **Georgij Nemirovič-Dančenko**, a Russian émigré who arrived in Munich on 25 Feb 1921 with his wife Anna (née **Uljanska**, married November 1915). This match is probable, not yet confirmed by a birth record.

The Bruck/Koschembahr ancestry below Frederick is your mom's step-family heritage, not her blood ancestry.

- **His father died in 1939**, which matches Georgij's death in Tallinn. His mother then married Friedrich Koschembahr, who adopted him.

Anne Marie lived in Burgsteinfurt from childhood until 1948. The file doesn't name her parents.

### Georgij Nemirovič-Dančenko (1889 – 1939)
> **Correction:** the osmikon article names his father as Vladimir (1858–1943), the Moscow Art Theatre founder. Russian Wikipedia and Rodovid give **Vladimir Prokofievich** (1864–1896), a lieutenant colonel who was the director's first cousin. The family is noble, with the Gozdawa coat of arms, recorded in the Chernigov and Kazan nobility books, and descended from Danila Nemirich.

- **Early career.**
  - Born 7/19 Jul 1889 in St. Petersburg. His mother was Ekaterina (Katharina Bertha), née Junge.
  - Graduated top of his class from the Imperial School of Jurisprudence in 1910.
  - Joined the Imperial Council in 1910 and became a Court Councillor in 1914.
- **The Civil War.** In 1917 he ran energy supply for the Odessa region. He then joined the anti-Bolshevik Whites and became press chief of General Wrangel's government in Crimea. His memoir of that time is *V Krymu pri Vrangele*.
- **Munich, 1921 to about 1927.** He worked as a writer, editor of the *Aufbau* correspondence and Ukraine expert of the Aufbau association. He was secretary of the Russian Monarchist Association in Bavaria, with Anna also active, and a supporter of Grand Duke Kirill's claim to the throne.
- **His politics.** He was close to Max Erwin von Scheubner-Richter. In April 1921 he spoke at an NSDAP event that Hitler chaired, and antisemitic rhetoric appears in his writing.
- **Later life.** He was penniless after 1924.
  - In 1932 he moved to Clichy, near Paris, and earned a law doctorate.
  - In France he joined the Young Russia (Mladorossi) movement, edited for *Illustrirovannaya Rossiya* and became a Freemason.
  - He died in Tallinn on 23 Aug 1939.
  - He also wrote for the *Völkischer Beobachter* and, later, *Novoe Slovo* (Russian Wikipedia).
- **Source:** osmikon.de, "Networking for the Monarchy – The Munich Years of Georgij (Georg) Nemirovič-Dančenko". You read it and pasted the text, translated.

## How the surname came about
The Bruck/Koschembahr family's direct male line is **Bruck**, not Koschembahr. Wilhelm Bruck married the noblewoman Mathilde Margarethe von Koschembahr in 1884, and the couple used "Bruck von Koschembahr". After he died in 1907, she went back to her maiden name.

Their son Gerhard formally took the name in 1924, through an "adoption" by his unmarried aunt Mathilde von Koschembahr (1866–1931). The family dropped "Bruck" by about 1934. Frederick's line in America used "Von Koschembahr" too.

The von Koschembahr line itself is old Silesian nobility, documented from about 1564 (Hans Kossenbohr, called Skorkowski, Herr auf Skorkau).
